Problem Statement Medium
LeetCode 1248

Given an array of integers nums and an integer k. A continuous subarray is called nice if there are k odd numbers on it.

Return the number of nice sub-arrays.

Example 1: Input: nums = [1,1,2,1,1], k = 3 → Output: 2
The only sub-arrays with 3 odd numbers are [1,1,2,1] and [1,2,1,1].
Example 2: Input: nums = [2,4,6], k = 1 → Output: 0 (There are no odd numbers in the array)
Example 3: Input: nums = [2,2,2,1,2,2,1,2,2,2], k = 2 → Output: 16
Constraints: 1 ≤ nums.length ≤ 50000, 1 ≤ nums[i] ≤ 105, 1 ≤ k ≤ nums.length.
Algorithm Summary & Parity Reduction Proof
O(1) Aux Space
Binary Parity Equivalence (LeetCode 930 ↔ 1248): Every odd number contributes 1 to oddCount (nums[i] % 2 != 0) and every even number contributes 0. Thus, finding subarrays with exact k odd numbers is identical to subtracting subarrays with ≤ k - 1 odds from subarrays with ≤ k odds:
exact(k) = atMost(nums, k) − atMost(nums, k - 1)
Why count += right - left + 1 Works in Ο(N): After shrinking left until oddCount ≤ k, every subarray ending at right and starting at any index from left to right has at most k odd integers — giving right - left + 1 valid subarrays in Ο(1).

Ο(N)

Two linear sliding-window passes (2 × 2N = Ο(N)).

Space Complexity
