Problem Statement

Dynamic Programming
LeetCode 122 • 2-State DP

You are given an integer array prices of length n, where prices[i] is the price of a given stock on the ith day. On each day, you may decide to buy, sell, or do nothing. Find and print the maximum total profit you can achieve across multiple transactions.

📌Trading Rules & Constraints
  • • At Most One Share: You may hold at most one stock at any time—you must sell your current stock before buying again.
  • • Unlimited Transactions: You may buy and sell as many times as you want (even buying and selling on the same day if optimal).
  • • State Definitions: Track cash (max profit when not holding) and hold (max profit when holding 1 stock).
  • • Edge Cases: If n == 0 or prices only fall, no profitable trade exists and the answer is 0.
Input Format (Assumed)
Line 1: n (number of days)
Line 2: prices[0] prices[1] ... prices[n-1]
Output Format
Single integer: maximum profit (cash)
Base (Day 0): cash = 0, hold = -prices[0]
Worked Examples
Ex 1: 6 / 7 1 5 3 6 4 → 7 (Buy @ 1, Sell @ 5 [+4]; Buy @ 3, Sell @ 6 [+3] → 4 + 3 = 7)
Ex 2: 5 / 1 2 3 4 5 → 4 (Buy @ 1, Sell @ 5 → profit 4)
Ex 3: 5 / 7 6 4 3 1 → 0 (Prices strictly fall; never buy → profit 0)

Algorithm Summary & Complexity Analysis

O(n) Time • O(1) Space
🎯1. Why Use a 2-State DP Machine?

Trying every possible combination of buy, sell, and skip across n days takes exponential O(2n) time. Instead of remembering the entire past history, notice that on any day your future options depend on only one question: "Am I currently holding a stock or not?" Modeling this as two states (cash and hold) collapses all overlapping paths into O(n) time and easily extends to harder variants (transaction fees, cooldowns, or at-most-K trades).

⚙️2. How It Works Day-by-Day (State Transitions):
  • Day 0 Initialization: Start with cash = 0 (do nothing, $0 profit) and hold = -prices[0] (buy Day 0's stock, so your balance drops by prices[0]).
  • Snapshot Safety (previousCash, previousHold): At the start of each day i, we save yesterday's values so both updates read strictly from day i - 1 without today's new cash interfering with today's hold.
  • Update cash = max(previousCash, previousHold + prices[i]): To end day i with no stock, pick the better of: Rest (keep yesterday's previousCash) or Sell the stock you held yesterday and collect +prices[i].
  • Update hold = max(previousHold, previousCash - prices[i]): To end day i holding 1 stock, pick the better of: Keep yesterday's stock (previousHold) or Buy today using yesterday's cash (-prices[i]).
  • Final Answer (System.out.println(cash)): Holding an unsold stock on the last day can never give more profit than having sold it, so cash always holds the maximum possible profit.
TIME COMPLEXITY
O(n)
Single linear pass over n days, performing two O(1) Math.max transitions per day.
AUXILIARY SPACE
O(1)
Since day i only needs day i - 1, the full dp[n][2] table compresses to 4 scalar ints.
